# Demand Pass — 15 Aug 2026

## Purpose

Test whether buyers in each candidate vertical have noticed an AI visibility
problem. Read alongside the 13 Aug Dubai answer-surface scan. Demand breaks ties
per `decisions/2026-08-15-vertical-shortlist-revision.md`.

---

## CORRECTION LOGGED

An earlier draft of this pass recorded "no UAE-specific buyer data exists" as a
finding. That was false. No UAE-qualified searches had been run — the gap was
untested, not real. Corrected below. The error is recorded rather than removed
because it is the same failure mode as reasoning forward from a single new fact:
an absence in the results was reported as an absence in the world.

---

## FINDING 1 — the instrument returned something other than what it was built to find

The pass was specified to count buyer-side complaint instances (Reddit, LinkedIn,
trade forums), classified 1–4, against ratified bands.

**Almost no buyer-side complaint content exists, in any vertical.** What the pass
found in volume instead was **supply-side saturation** — specialist AEO agencies,
tools and benchmark reports already targeting every vertical on the shortlist,
publishing original research and quoting retainer pricing.

**Bands not applied.** The threshold (15 instances, 5+ in classes 1–2) was designed
for an instrument that returned near-zero signal in every vertical, including ones
with overwhelming independent evidence of demand. Applying it would have failed all
six. Bands retained for future use where forum signal exists; this pass is recorded
as a substituted instrument, not a band evaluation.

## FINDING 2 — the UAE is a high-adoption market, not a lagging one

The most commercially significant finding in the pass.

| Source | Finding |
|---|---|
| Microsoft (AI diffusion, 2026) | UAE and Singapore highest globally; >60% of population used a generative AI tool in the past year |
| PwC Middle East | 58% of UAE/KSA consumers have used tools like ChatGPT or Gemini — markedly above European markets; 55% of users engage weekly or daily |
| DHL *eCommerce Trends Report 2026* (29,000 shoppers, 5,800 businesses, 29 countries) | 51% of UAE shoppers use AI chat tools when shopping online — behind only India, ahead of China |
| Checkout.com *Agentic Commerce 2026* | 79% of UAE consumers comfortable letting AI complete a purchase; 71% would let an AI agent substitute their preferred brand for better value |
| Eight Oh Two *2026 AI and Search Behavior Study* (UAE, n=500 active AI users, Nov 2025) | 37% now start searches with AI rather than Google; 85% still cross-check answers |
| PwC Middle East Workforce Survey 2025 | 75% of Middle East employees used AI tools at work in the past year, vs 69% globally |

**Positioning consequence.** Not "AEO specialists haven't reached this market yet."
The sharper, evidenced version: **the market with among the world's highest AI
adoption has almost no AEO specialists operating in it.** Every competitor
identified in this pass operates in the US or UK, where consumer adoption is lower.

**Urgency argument for pitches.** If 71% of UAE consumers will let an AI substitute
their preferred brand for better value, incumbency does not protect a prospect.
Brand loyalty is a weaker defence here than in the markets the research usually
describes.

**Reliability note.** Use DHL, PwC and Microsoft as load-bearing citations. Eight
Oh Two samples active AI users only and is published by a UAE agency (supply side).
Checkout.com's trust figures are high enough to suspect social-desirability effect.

## MARKET STRATEGY — UAE first, US/UK as the growth market

Saturation was initially treated as disqualifying for US/UK. That reasoning is
corrected here.

**Where the earlier read was right:** the competitors found are not enterprise-only.
They already operate at mid-market — one runs AI-visibility audits on individual
manufacturers, aesthetics AEO is quoted at $2,000–8,000/month. The mid-market gap
in US/UK is smaller than assumed.

**Where it was wrong:** at a solo capacity ceiling of roughly 6–8 retainer clients,
competitor density is close to irrelevant. The requirement is not market share, it
is six clients. A larger pool makes six easier to find, saturated or not.

**The resolving factor is credibility, not competition.** In US/UK, Brandure
competes against specialists holding published case studies and original benchmark
research, with none of its own. In the UAE it competes against nobody. That makes
this a sequence, not a choice:

1. **UAE first** — win the first two or three clients where no credibility is
   required to get a meeting, and where the 13 Aug Dubai scan is proprietary
   evidence no competitor holds.
2. **US/UK mid-market second** — businesses with enough budget for specialist
   marketing but not enough for full-service agency retainers. Entered once
   documented case studies exist.

**Delivery consideration supporting the same order:** earned-media placement
requires relationships with publications in the target market. UAE placement is
reachable from Dubai; US/UK placement is not, initially.

## VERTICAL FINDINGS

### Aesthetic clinics / health & wellness
- NewBeauty *State of Aesthetics 2026*: 30.3% of patients have used AI to research
  treatments — ahead of YouTube (20.4%), approaching online forums (38.6%).
- 5WPR / Haute MD *Medical Aesthetics AI Visibility Index 2026* (Apr 2026): 60+
  patient-intent queries across ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews.
- OpenAI (Jan 2026, secondary reporting): health a large and growing share of
  ChatGPT usage; majority of health conversations occur outside clinic hours.
- **Supply side:** active. Specialist aesthetics AEO agencies publishing playbooks.
  Observed pricing $2,000–8,000/month.
- **Structural ceiling:** two pharmaceutical manufacturers reportedly hold ~80% of
  aesthetic-medicine AI citations. Individual clinics compete for the remainder.
  State this in a pitch rather than be caught by it.
- **Dubai scan:** four surfaces returned four disjoint clinic lists. No local
  incumbent. Live entity-disambiguation problem (SKIN111 vs SKINIII).

### B2B SaaS
- Forrester *2026 Buyers' Journey Survey* (~18,000 global business buyers, Jan
  2026): 94% used AI during their most recent purchase, up from 89%. 55% compared
  vendors inside AI tools, 54% researched products, 47% built internal business
  cases before contacting a vendor. AI answer engines reported as outranking vendor
  websites, sales reps and product experts.
- Reported B2B traffic declines of 10–40% as research migrates into AI engines.
- **Supply side:** most contested service market of the seven.
- **Dubai scan:** category definition openly unstable; Bayzat winning two surfaces
  via a self-published listicle. Mechanic proven and transferable.

### Industrial manufacturers
- Gardner *2025 Industrial Buying Influence Survey*: 26% of manufacturing buyers
  select AI Overview as a top-two preferred search return type, up from 8% in 2024.
  6% trust AI output directly; 55% verify against other sources; 9% click through to
  cited sources; **31% do not use AI for work research at all** — lowest adoption of
  any vertical tested.
- Magenta Associates (2025): 66% of UK senior decision-makers use AI in supplier
  research — 85% among 25–34s, 23% among over-55s.
- The 55%-verify figure is operative: AI is the shortlist stage, verification
  follows. Absence from the shortlist removes a supplier before verification.
- **Buyer-side complaints:** none found. Consistent with the pre-registered caveat
  on low posting volume. Inconclusive, not negative.
- **Supply side:** active since at least April 2026. One US agency reports auditing
  11 manufacturers Jun–Jul 2026, finding every one invisible for at least one core
  buying question.
- **Dubai scan:** zero citations on ChatGPT's answer, near-zero cross-surface
  overlap, local pack leaders with 7–35 reviews. Most winnable answer surface.

### Legal / professional services
- ~68% of legal-related queries reported to trigger AI Overviews (US).
- Overlap between organic top-10 ranking and AI Overview citation reported to have
  fallen from ~75% (mid-2025) to 17–38% by early 2026, varying by query class.
- Legal sits in Google's YMYL category — higher citation threshold than most.
- **Supply side:** saturated. Six of eight results returned were legal-AEO agency
  marketing; at least one competitor publishing original benchmark research.
- **Dubai scan:** three firms already running self-published listicles that AI
  surfaces are grounding on. The play works locally and is being run.

### Automotive / car dealerships
- Ekho *2026 AI Vehicle Research Study*: 30% of vehicle buyers used a generative AI
  tool during research; 68.4% of those used ChatGPT.
- Cox Automotive: 19% of all buyers, 25% of new-vehicle buyers, using AI sites or AI
  overviews.
- Savvy Dealer study of 100 US dealer sites: 84 scored below 60/100 on AI
  visibility; average 34; only 14 scored 80+.
- **Counter-signal:** C-4 Analytics reports most dealerships receive **under 2% of
  total traffic** from AI platforms. High research usage, low referral traffic. The
  commercial case rests on shortlist influence, not clicks — a harder sell.
- **Supply side:** heaviest of the seven. Dedicated dealership AEO agencies, tools,
  quarterly benchmarks and prompt libraries already in market.

### Boutique hospitality — deprioritised 13 Aug
Genuine third-party consensus layer (TripAdvisor, Visit Dubai, Google Hotels).
Competing means displacing OTAs. Compounds with the ability-to-pay concern.

### Higher education — deprioritised 13 Aug
Anchored to QS, Times Higher Education and KHDA. Highest cross-surface consensus of
any category. Cannot outrank a ranking body.

## SCORING

Scored out of 5 on each axis. **Prompt** = answer-surface opportunity from the
13 Aug Dubai scan. **Demand** = buyer adoption and commercial case from this pass.

| Rank | Vertical | Prompt | Demand | Total | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Aesthetic clinics** | 5 | 4 | **9** | Zero cross-surface consensus in Dubai; no incumbent. Ceiling set by pharma citation dominance. |
| 2 | **B2B SaaS** | 4 | 5 | **9** | Strongest demand data of any vertical; most contested service market. |
| 3 | **Industrial manufacturers** | 5 | 3 | **8** | Most winnable surface; 31% of buyers don't use AI at all; client awareness unevidenced. |
| 4 | **Legal / professional** | 3 | 4 | **7** | Mechanic demonstrated locally but three firms already running it. Window closing fastest. |
| 5 | **Automotive** | 3 | 3 | **6** | Real buyer adoption, weak traffic case, heaviest competition. |
| 6 | **Boutique hospitality** | 2 | 2 | **4** | Consensus layer exists; ability to pay poor. |
| 7 | **Higher education** | 1 | 2 | **3** | Institutional anchors; cannot be moved. |

**Tie-break, rank 1 vs 2 — aesthetic clinics takes it.** Both score 9. B2B SaaS has
better demand data, but that data is global and so is its competition — Brandure
would be competing with established US agencies for a globally distributed buyer,
holding no case studies. Aesthetics is geographically defensible: the buyer is
local, the Dubai scan is proprietary evidence, delivery leans on Google Business
Profile and local earned media rather than US publication relationships, and there
is no local incumbent on any of the four surfaces.

**Recommended first vertical: aesthetic clinics (UAE).**

## METHOD CHANGE (proposed, for ratification)

Buyer-side complaint scraping does not work for this category. Marketing
decision-makers rarely post publicly about visibility problems, in any vertical.

- **Primary instrument:** trade and industry survey data. Answered the channel
  question in roughly three searches per vertical and produces figures usable in
  client-facing material.
- **Secondary:** forum and social scraping, where signal exists.
- **Third output, now explicit:** supply-side competitor mapping. The highest-value
  unplanned output of this pass.
- **Mandatory:** every search set must be run market-qualified. The UAE gap in the
  first draft was caused by unqualified queries defaulting to US/UK results.

---

## CAVEATS

- Forrester, Gardner, Cox, Magenta and Microsoft figures reached via **secondary
  reporting**, not primary documents. Verify before client-facing use.
- Supply-side content is self-interested. Figures published by AEO agencies favour
  the case for AEO. Trade-body and independent survey figures carry more weight.
- UAE data is **market-level, not vertical-level**. No UAE-specific figures were
  found for aesthetics, legal, SaaS, manufacturing or automotive buyer behaviour
  individually. The 13 Aug Dubai scan remains the only vertical-level UAE evidence
  held.
- Higher education and boutique hospitality not researched in this pass.
- Single pass, one researcher, public web only.
