# Vertical shortlist revision

Date: 2026-08-15

**Amends `decisions/2026-08-08-vertical-shortlist.md`. Supersedes nothing.**
The original shortlist, its selection criteria and its reasoning all stand
except where changed below. Read both together.

Evidence base: `research/vertical-sprint/scans/2026-08-13-dubai/`, a four-surface
scan of `Best [category] in Dubai` across seven categories on 13 August 2026.

**Standing caveat on that evidence.** Half the scan is contaminated: Gemini and
Google Search ran on Joe's signed-in Chrome profile, and all four surfaces ran
at n=1 on free tiers. Only ChatGPT and Perplexity are clean. Every conclusion
below is drawn from the clean surfaces only, or is explicitly held pending
re-run.

---

## Decided — boutique and independent hospitality moves to deprioritised

From primary 4 of 4 to deprioritised, alongside universities.

**Evidence.** ChatGPT and Perplexity — both clean, uncontaminated surfaces —
ground their boutique hotel answers on Visit Dubai and Tripadvisor. That
indicates a genuine third-party consensus layer: an established set of sources
the models already trust for this category. Competing means displacing OTAs and
established travel publishers on their own ground, which is a different and much
harder job than filling a vacuum.

This is the strongest form of evidence the scan produced, because it holds on
the two surfaces that were not contaminated. It does not depend on the Gemini
or Google Search cells at all.

**It compounds with a concern already on record.** The original shortlist
flagged lower value per client as this vertical's known risk and noted it might
require a productised offer rather than a retainer. An independent boutique
hotel is unlikely to fund a retainer at Brandure's price floor — and the price
floor is capacity-bound, not market-bound, per the sizing model in
`research/sources/2026-08-12-donnelly-searchable-playbook.md`. A hard category
to win and a client who cannot pay for winning it is not a marginal call.

**Consequence.** `research/vertical-sprint/README.md` shortlist updated. The
hospitality prompt template stays in place, marked deprioritised — the work is
done and costs nothing to keep, and this decision could reverse.

## Not decided — industrial manufacturers held pending the demand pass

**The scan recommends promoting industrial manufacturers to first-priority
vertical. Not actioned.**

The scan measures a supply-side vacuum: no authoritative source, no
cross-surface consensus, local pack entries with 7–35 reviews, only one brand
appearing on more than one surface. That is a real and well-evidenced finding
about the answer surface.

**It infers commercial opportunity from that vacuum, and the inference does not
follow.** The scan does not test whether buyers in this category use AI search
to find suppliers. Industrial B2B procurement typically runs on trade shows,
existing relationships and procurement lists.

An absence of authority is consistent with two opposite readings:

1. **Nobody is competing yet** — an open goal, and the highest-leverage vertical
   in the set.
2. **AI is not a discovery channel for this category** — the vacuum exists
   because nobody looks, and filling it wins nothing.

**Both look identical in this dataset.** No amount of further scanning
distinguishes them, because scanning measures the supply side and the question
is about the demand side.

**The demand pass is the instrument that separates them.**
`research/vertical-sprint/demand-signals.md` tests whether buyers are
complaining, in their own words, unprompted. Applied here, reading 1 predicts
buyer-side instances in signal classes 1–2; reading 2 predicts near-silence.
Resolve after it runs, against the ratified bands.

This is the case the two-axis framing in `demand-signals.md` was written for —
a wide-open answer surface with unknown buyer demand — and it arrived faster
than expected.

## No change — aesthetic clinics remain primary

The scan confirms rather than contradicts the existing shortlist. Four surfaces
returned four disjoint sets of clinics with no brand appearing on more than
one, which is consistent with the contestable answer surface the original
shortlist predicted for this category.

Note that this finding is one of the two the scan itself flags for re-running at
n=3 — see action A3 in the archived actions list. The conclusion is unchanged
either way, because aesthetic clinics were already primary on independent
grounds. What the re-run affects is whether the disjointness claim can be used
externally, not whether the vertical stays on the list.

## Also unchanged

Legal and professional services, and B2B SaaS, remain primary. The scan's
listicle finding — brands publishing "10 Best X in Dubai" posts and being
ingested as neutral authority — is drawn substantially from clean surfaces and
supports both. It is a finding about **method**, not about vertical selection,
and belongs in Section B of the archived actions list, which is not lifted.

## What would reverse it

- **Demand pass evidence on hospitality.** The deprioritisation rests on the
  consensus layer plus the price floor. Strong buyer-side complaint volume from
  boutique operators would not remove the consensus layer, but it would
  reopen the question of whether a productised fixed-price offer works where a
  retainer does not.
- **Demand pass evidence on industrial manufacturers**, which is the point of
  holding it rather than deciding it.
- **The A2 and A3 re-runs materially changing the scan.** The hospitality
  finding is drawn from clean surfaces and should survive, but a corrected scan
  that contradicts it would put this decision back to the original shortlist.
- **A first client landing in a deprioritised vertical and going well.** Same
  reversal condition as the original record: revenue and a reference case beat
  a shortlist.
