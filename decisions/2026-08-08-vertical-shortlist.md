# Vertical shortlist

Date: 2026-08-08

## Decision

Test four primary verticals before committing to an ICP, in this order:

1. Legal and professional services
2. B2B SaaS
3. Health, wellness and aesthetics
4. Boutique and independent hospitality

Reserve list, tested only if the primaries disappoint: manufacturing and
industrial B2B, higher education, automotive.

The shortlist is a testing order, not a positioning claim. Brandure is not
announcing a vertical specialism until the sprint produces evidence. Method is
in `research/vertical-sprint/README.md`.

## Reasoning

A solo agency cannot sell horizontally. Vertical focus makes the prospecting
list finite, the competitor set reusable across clients, and the citation map
partly transferable — which is the difference between an hour of delivery and
an asset that amortises.

The shortlist was selected against four criteria: research-heavy buying journey
(so the buyer actually consults an AI answer before deciding), high value per
client (so a small book of clients is viable), a contestable answer surface
(incumbents present but beatable, rather than a category where models default
to two dominant brands), and access — categories Joe can credibly reach from
Dubai.

Why each primary:

- **Legal and professional services.** High value per client, a genuinely
  research-heavy selection process, and a category where First Page Sage found
  among the largest ChatGPT conversion gains. The risk is that directories and
  review aggregators dominate the citation surface — which the sprint is
  designed to detect, and which if true is itself the sellable problem.
- **B2B SaaS.** Fluent buyers who already understand the problem and need no
  education on why AI answers matter, plus a comparison-heavy purchase pattern
  that maps directly to answer inclusion. Penetration sits at 28%, lower than
  hospitality, but buyer sophistication and willingness to pay compensate. The
  risk is that it is the most crowded category for anyone selling AEO.
- **Health, wellness and aesthetics.** Strong in Dubai specifically, high
  margins, and a category where the buyer asks a model for a recommendation and
  frequently acts on it. The risk is regulatory sensitivity around claims.
- **Boutique and independent hospitality.** Travel and hospitality leads AI
  purchase-journey penetration at 47%, and First Page Sage found hotels and
  resorts among the top conversion-gain categories. Independents are chosen
  over chains: chains have in-house teams, independents have budget and no
  capability. The risk is lower value per client, which may require a
  productised offer rather than a retainer.

Reserves are held back rather than dropped. Manufacturing and industrial B2B
appear in First Page Sage's high-gain list, and higher education ranks high on
both that list and on research intensity — but access is harder from Dubai and
sales cycles are long. Automotive is included for local market reasons.

Categories explicitly deprioritised: financial services, engineering and heavy
equipment, all of which First Page Sage found among the smallest conversion
gains.

## What would reverse it

- Sprint evidence that a primary vertical's answer surface is locked — the same
  two or three brands cited across every prompt and model, with no visible
  route in. That vertical drops to the reserve list.
- Sprint evidence that review sites and directories completely own a category's
  citations. This is not automatically disqualifying — it may be the sellable
  problem — but it changes the service being sold from content and entity work
  to third-party placement, and needs a decision before it is offered.
- A first client landing in a reserve or unlisted vertical and going well.
  Revenue and a reference case beat a shortlist derived from secondary research.
- Newer data superseding the First Page Sage and penetration figures the
  shortlist leans on. Those are the basis for the ordering; if they move, the
  ordering should be re-derived rather than defended.
