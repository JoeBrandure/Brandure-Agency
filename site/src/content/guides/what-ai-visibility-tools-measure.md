AI visibility tools tell you where your business stands in AI-generated answers right now. They do not change that position. Understanding the difference between measuring visibility and improving it is the most important thing a business owner or marketing lead can do before deciding how to spend time and budget on [answer engine optimisation (AEO)](/guides/answer-engine-optimisation-uk-uae-guide).

**The short answer:** a tool gives you the score. The work that changes the score sits elsewhere: on your website, in your entity data, in your content, and in the third-party sources that ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews and Perplexity choose to cite. This guide explains what each metric means, what the tools do well, what each option costs, and what has to happen to make the numbers move.

## What do AI visibility tools actually measure?

Most tools in this category track a consistent set of metrics across the engines you care about. The terminology varies by product, but the underlying measurements are broadly the same.

| Metric | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Mention rate** | The percentage of relevant prompts in which your brand is named at all |
| **Share of voice** | Your mentions as a proportion of all brand mentions across those same prompts |
| **Citation share** | How often your website or content is linked as a source in AI answers |
| **Sentiment** | Whether the engine's language about your brand is positive, neutral or negative |
| **Prompt coverage** | Which question types or topic areas trigger your brand to appear |
| **Engine breakdown** | How each metric differs between ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews and Perplexity |

The engine breakdown matters more than most businesses expect. [Google's help page on AI Overviews](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/14901683) describes AI Overviews as an AI-generated snapshot with links to supporting information from the web. Perplexity cites sources directly and visibly. ChatGPT draws on training data and, in browsing mode, live retrieval. Because the three engines work differently, a business can rank well in one and be invisible in another. A good tool surfaces that gap engine by engine, not as a blended average.

**Key point:** the metrics above describe your current state. None of them, on their own, tell you why you are where you are or what to do about it.

## What AI visibility tools do well

Used correctly, AI visibility tools are genuinely useful. Brandure uses them as part of every client engagement, and they are worth using for any business that wants to understand its position in AI-generated answers.

### They give you a baseline you can track over time

Without a measurement, you cannot know whether the work you are doing is having any effect. A tool gives you a starting point and a way to compare month on month. That is not a small thing.

### They show you where competitors are appearing

If a competitor is named in 60% of prompts in your category and you appear in 12%, you know the gap. You do not need to guess whether there is a problem.

### They identify which engines need the most attention

Because ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews and Perplexity behave differently, your scores will differ across them. A tool that breaks results down by engine tells you where to prioritise effort rather than spreading it evenly across all three.

### They surface prompt coverage gaps

If your business appears when someone asks "best accountants in Dubai" but not when they ask "who handles VAT returns for SMEs in the UAE", that gap is a content and entity problem. A tool can show you which prompt types are missing. Fixing them is a separate task.

## What AI visibility tools do not do

This is the distinction that matters most, and it is the one most often glossed over in tool marketing. Measuring visibility and changing visibility are two entirely different activities.

| What a tool does | What a tool does not do |
| --- | --- |
| Reports your mention rate across engines | Increases your mention rate |
| Shows which competitors are being named | Removes competitors from those answers |
| Identifies prompt coverage gaps | Creates the content that fills those gaps |
| Flags negative sentiment | Corrects the entity data or sources driving it |
| Tracks citation share over time | Earns the third-party placements that build citation share |
| Breaks down results by engine | Changes how any engine retrieves or weights your brand |

A tool is a diagnostic instrument. It tells you what is wrong in the same way a blood test tells a doctor what to investigate. The blood test does not treat the condition.

[Search Engine Land's coverage of AI search](https://searchengineland.com/category/ai-search/) consistently makes the point that AI answer engines are drawing on a broader set of signals than traditional search: structured data, entity relationships, third-party sources and content quality all contribute. A tool can surface which of those signals are weak. Addressing them requires execution.

## What actually moves the numbers?

AI visibility improves when the engines have better reasons to name and cite your business. That comes down to four areas of work.

### Website foundations

AI crawlers need to be able to access and understand your site. That means correct robots.txt permissions, structured data markup (particularly schema.org types relevant to your business category), and pages that answer questions directly rather than burying information in marketing copy. [Google's structured data documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/intro-structured-data) is the authoritative reference for what the schema markup should look like and how it is processed. Google says its AI features need no special markup beyond normal search best practice, so treat structured data as a clarity aid, not a shortcut.

### Entity data

AI engines build a model of what your business is: its name, location, category, services, founding date and relationships to other entities. If that data is inconsistent across your website, Google Business Profile, industry directories and third-party listings, the engines have less confidence in naming you. Entity correction means making those signals consistent and complete.

### Answer-ready content

Content that moves the numbers answers the specific questions buyers ask AI tools. That is different from content written to rank in traditional search. The format matters: direct answers in the first sentence, clear headings, concise paragraphs.

### Third-party sources

ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews and Perplexity do not rely only on your own website. They pull from directories, review platforms, industry publications and news sites. Our research found that across the AI engines we tested, only 10 of the 147 Dubai businesses named appeared in every engine's answers. In our testing, the engines only agreed where a strong third-party authority, such as a recognised directory or ranking, existed in the category. Earned placements on those sources, with consistent and accurate information about your business, are one of the strongest signals available. This is not something a tool can automate. It requires outreach, relationship-building and, in some cases, content creation for external platforms.

## When is a tool enough, and when do you need agency support?

A tool works well when you already have people who can act on what it tells you. If your team includes someone who can update schema markup, brief and publish content, manage third-party listings and track changes over time, a tool gives them exactly what they need to prioritise that work.

Most businesses do not have that capacity in-house. The tool surfaces the problem; the capacity to fix it has to come from somewhere, whether that is a skilled internal team or an agency that handles the execution.

Tools and agencies do different jobs, so the useful question is: **once we know what is wrong, who is going to fix it?**

> If your answer is "we are not sure", that is a strong signal that measurement alone will not move your numbers.

For businesses that want to understand where they stand before committing to a programme of work, a starting point is a structured AI visibility audit that identifies the specific gaps across ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews and Perplexity, and maps them to the fixes that would have the most impact. That is a different output from a tool dashboard; it is an actionable plan. ## How should a business evaluate AI visibility progress?

Progress in AI visibility is slower than paid advertising and faster than traditional SEO, typically. Changes to entity data and on-site content can begin to affect results within four to eight weeks. Earned third-party placements take longer to be indexed and weighted. These are typical ranges, not guarantees. Timing depends on your starting visibility, the engine, the type of work and how competitive your category is, which is why we measure your starting position first.

### What to track, and how often

- **Mention rate by engine:** check monthly. Look for movement in ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews and Perplexity separately, not as a combined figure.
- **Prompt coverage:** review quarterly. Are you appearing in more question types than you were three months ago?
- **Citation share:** track monthly. An increase here usually follows successful third-party placement work.
- **Sentiment:** flag any negative shift immediately. It often points to a specific source or piece of content that needs attention.

### What a meaningful result looks like

A business starting from low visibility should expect to see measurable movement in mention rate and prompt coverage within the first two to three months of active work. Share of voice and citation share tend to follow as third-party placements accumulate.

**Flat numbers after three months of execution are a signal**, not a reason to keep waiting. Either the work being done is not targeting the right signals, or the volume of activity is insufficient. A good measurement tool will show you which metrics are moving and which are not, so you can adjust.

## The bottom line

AI visibility tools are a legitimate and useful part of any AEO programme. They give you data you cannot get any other way, and they make it possible to track progress objectively over time. Knowing your score does not get you named. The businesses that appear consistently across ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews and Perplexity have done the structural, content and citation work that gives the engines a reason to name them.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between AI visibility and traditional SEO?**
Traditional SEO focuses on ranking in a list of search results. AI visibility refers to whether your business is named, recommended or cited in the conversational answers that ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews and Perplexity generate. The signals that drive each overlap but are not identical.

**Can I improve AI visibility without using a tracking tool?**
You can do the work without a tool, but you will have no reliable way to know whether it is having an effect. A tracking tool gives you the baseline and the ongoing measurement that makes the programme accountable.

**How long does it take to see results from AEO work?**
Entity and on-site changes can begin to show up in AI answers within four to eight weeks. Third-party citation work takes longer, typically three to six months for meaningful movement in citation share and share of voice. These are typical ranges, not guarantees: timing depends on your starting visibility, the engine, the type of work and how competitive your category is.

**Do AI visibility tools work differently for businesses in the UK versus the UAE?**
The metrics are the same, but the prompt sets and competitor landscape differ by market. A tool needs to be configured with prompts relevant to your geography and category, not generic global queries, to give you useful data.

**Is a high score in one engine enough?**
No. ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews and Perplexity draw on different signals and have different user bases. A business that appears in one engine but not the others is missing a significant share of AI-influenced buyer decisions.
